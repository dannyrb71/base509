## The short version

Your business account belongs to your business — not to whoever happens to be logged in. This page says who controls an account, what happens when people leave, and what we'll do if there's a dispute. **We won't referee your business.**

---

## 1. "Owner" is a permission level — not legal ownership

> **⚠️ v0.2 correction (2026-07-16):** an earlier draft said *"the person who creates the business account is its Owner."* **That was wrong and dangerous** — it would let an unauthorized employee who signs up first claim control of a business. **"Owner" is the highest administrative permission level inside PetAppro. It does not determine or establish legal ownership of the business, its assets, records, or client relationships.** Whoever legally owns the business owns it, regardless of who holds the Owner permission in our app.

The person who creates a business account **represents that they are authorized by that business to do so**, and is assigned the **Owner permission level**. The Owner permission:

- controls the subscription and billing,
- invites staff and sets their permission levels (Staff · Manager · Admin),
- can transfer ownership to another Admin or Owner,
- and is responsible for what the business does in PetAppro.

**Roles below Owner don't own the account.** An Admin can see financial data and manage operations — but an Admin isn't the Owner and can't take the business over.

**The business owns the business data** — the client list, pets, bookings, notes, and invoices. Staff members don't take it with them when they go.

---

## 2. Personal accounts vs. the business

Your **personal account** (your identity, your login) is yours. The **business account** is the business's. Leaving a business — or having your access removed — doesn't delete your personal account, and deleting your personal account doesn't automatically delete the business. See §5.

If you work for more than one provider, you have **one** personal account with access to each of them.

---

## 3. Adding and removing people

- The Owner (and Admins, where permitted) can invite and remove staff at any time.
- Removing someone ends their access immediately. Their work history stays with the business — the report cards they wrote, the bookings they ran.
- Seats count against your plan. Removing someone frees a seat.

---

## 4. Transferring ownership

An Owner can transfer ownership to another Owner or Admin on the account, from the portal. After transfer, the previous Owner keeps whatever role they're given — or none.

**Do this before you need it.** If you're a solo provider, adding a trusted second Admin now means your business isn't stranded if something happens to you.

## 5. When an Owner leaves or deletes their account

A business account holds records belonging to **other people** — clients, their pets, their booking history, and invoices. So we can't simply delete everything when an Owner leaves.

When an Owner deletes their personal account:

- **If the business has another Owner or Admin:** we ask you to **transfer ownership** to them first. The business continues; your personal account and personal information are deleted.
- **If there's nobody to transfer to:** the business is **closed**. Clients are notified that the business is no longer taking bookings. Personal information is deleted.
- **Either way:** a limited set of records may be retained where we are legally entitled or required to keep them for a disclosed purpose, **minimized and disconnected from any live account**. ## 6. If we can't reach the Owner — or someone disputes who the Owner should be

**We will not hand control of an account to someone who simply says it should be theirs.** We can't verify who really runs your company, and guessing wrong would do more damage than doing nothing.

If a dispute arises, we may temporarily restrict changes or access while we ask for documentation. We will transfer or restore the Owner permission **only** on one of the following (Danny, 2026-07-16 — deliberately narrow):

1. **A binding court order.** Always sufficient.
2. **Government business registration** identifying who is authorized to act for the business (e.g. a Secretary of State filing naming the managing member or officer), **matched to a verified identity**.
3. **Verified written instruction from the business**, signed by a person shown to be authorized.

We don't adjudicate ownership disputes. Sort it out between yourselves or through counsel; we'll follow a valid order.

## 7. Disputes between people at a business

**We don't referee.** If there's a disagreement about who controls a business account, sort it out between yourselves or through counsel. We'll follow a valid court order. Until then, the account stays as it is.

---

## 8. Deceased or incapacitated Owner

If an Owner dies or becomes unable to manage the account, we'll work with the authorized person — an executor, a successor, or someone with legal authority to act for the business — to transfer the account into the right hands, on appropriate documentation (such as a death certificate and evidence of authority).

This is exactly why we encourage every solo provider to **add a trusted second Admin now** (§4): it's the simplest way to make sure your business isn't stranded if something happens to you. If you need help here, email **support@base509.com** and we'll handle it with care.

## 9. If we suspend or terminate an account

If we suspend an account (non-payment, Terms violation), we'll tell the Owner why and what to do. **We won't hold your data hostage** — you'll have a reasonable window to export your records before anything is deleted. See [Cancellation](/policies/cancellation) §7.
