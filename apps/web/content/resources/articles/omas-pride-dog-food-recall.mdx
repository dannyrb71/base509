Oma’s Pride has voluntarily recalled one lot of its frozen raw **Woof Complete
Canine Chicken Recipe** after a sample collected and analyzed by the U.S. Food
and Drug Administration tested positive for *Salmonella*.

The FDA updated its announcement on August 17, 2026, to report that three dogs
had become ill in association with the complaint. No human illnesses had been
reported at the time of that update.

## How to identify the recalled food

According to the [FDA recall
announcement](https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/updated-omas-pride-voluntarily-recalls-one-lot-woof-complete-canine-chicken-recipe-6-lb-bag-because),
the recall is limited to:

- **Product:** Oma’s Pride Woof Complete Canine Chicken Recipe
- **Package:** 6 lb bag containing twelve individually wrapped 8 oz portions
- **Lot number:** BB012729
- **Item/SKU:** F-WOOFC-6
- **UPC:** 8 7938400145 9
- **Manufacturing date:** January 27, 2026
- **Best-by date:** January 27, 2029
- **Distribution dates:** February 12 through May 15, 2026

The company reported that 639 bags from this lot were distributed to retail and
wholesale accounts in Arizona, California, Indiana, Kentucky, Louisiana,
Maryland, New Jersey, Nevada, New York, Pennsylvania, and Virginia. The product
was also sold directly to consumers online and shipped frozen.

No other Oma’s Pride products, package sizes, or lots are included in the
recall, according to the FDA announcement.

## What to do if you use or encounter this food

If you have this food at home or find a potentially affected package while
caring for someone else’s dog:

1. **Stop before serving it.** Verify the package size and lot information.
2. **Keep anyone else from using it accidentally.** Do not sell, donate, or give
   the affected food to another household.
3. **If you are the provider, contact the client promptly.** Tell them exactly
   what you found and send a clear photograph of the package label and lot
   number.
4. **Do not improvise a replacement diet for a client’s dog.** Follow the
   owner’s instructions or the dog’s veterinarian-approved care plan. Owners
   with questions about an appropriate replacement should contact their
   veterinarian.
5. **Document the situation.** Record when the food was identified, whether any
   was served, whom you contacted, and the instructions you received.

The FDA advises consumers with an affected package to stop feeding it
immediately, dispose of it safely, and contact Oma’s Pride for a refund. The
company can be reached at **1-800-678-OMAS** or **hello@omaspride.com**.

## Clean anything the food may have touched

*Salmonella* can affect dogs that eat contaminated food. People can also be
exposed while handling the food or touching contaminated bowls, utensils,
containers, or surfaces.

The FDA recommends washing and sanitizing pet-food bowls, cups, and storage
containers, as well as washing and sanitizing hands after handling recalled food
or anything that contacted it. For a pet-care provider, that also means checking
food-preparation surfaces, scoops, refrigerator or freezer handles, and any
reusable transport containers involved in the feeding routine.

## Symptoms that call for professional care

The FDA says dogs with *Salmonella* infections may experience lethargy,
diarrhea or bloody diarrhea, fever, vomiting, decreased appetite, or abdominal
pain. Some infected dogs may appear otherwise healthy but can still carry and
spread the bacteria.

If a dog has eaten the recalled food and develops symptoms, notify the client
immediately and contact the dog’s veterinarian according to the client’s care
and emergency instructions.

People exposed to contaminated food can experience nausea, vomiting, diarrhea
or bloody diarrhea, abdominal cramping, and fever. Anyone experiencing symptoms
after exposure should contact a healthcare provider.

  A recall does not mean every package from a brand is affected. Share the

</Callout>

## A useful habit for every dog-owning household and pet-care business

Keep food in its original packaging or retain a photograph of the label, lot
number, and best-by date. Transferring food into an unmarked container may make
storage easier, but it can make a recall much harder to verify. Providers should
ask clients to preserve this information as part of their care instructions.

Providers should also maintain current feeding instructions, veterinarian and
emergency contacts, and a clear process for documenting unexpected care issues.
Fast, accurate communication protects the dog, reassures the client, and gives
everyone a reliable record of what happened.
